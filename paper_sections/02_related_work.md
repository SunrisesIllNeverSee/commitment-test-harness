# 2. Related Work

This work builds upon and extends several key areas of research in information theory, language model scaling, and agent architectures.

## 2.1 Foundational Information Theory
Shannon's seminal work established a mathematical theory of communication that quantifies reliable information transmission under noise without regard to semantic content. While this framework defines fundamental limits on channel capacity and error correction, it does not address which aspects of linguistic signals preserve identity under lossy transformation—a gap addressed by the present work. Our conservation principle extends Shannon's operational approach to the domain of signal identity under transformation rather than mere transmission.

## 2.2 Recent Advances in LLM Scaling and Internal Governance
Recent advances in large language model scaling have progressively exposed the limitations of ungoverned systems. Iterative deployment regimes enable emergent generalization and planning through self-curation and outer-loop feedback, while manifold-projected hyper-connections restore internal stability and scalability. Coordination frameworks identify missing System-2 layers for goal-directed coherence, and recursive self-invocation supports unbounded context and long-horizon tasks. Most recently, pure reinforcement learning has incentivized emergent self-reflection and test-time scaling without human-annotated traces. Collectively, these works provide elegant internal remedies for instability and scaling limits, yet leave unresolved the question of legitimacy and invariance preservation across multiple sovereign instances or recursive deployments.

## 2.3 Agent-Memory Frameworks and Their Limitations
Agent-memory frameworks such as SimpleMem demonstrate empirically that long-horizon performance depends critically on structured compression and recursive consolidation. Their ablation studies quantify substantial degradation when these components are disabled: temporal reasoning drops by 56.7% without structured compression (atomization), and multi-hop inference drops by 31.3% without consolidation. The system relies on explicit thresholds—an information threshold (τ = 0.35) to filter low-information content and a consolidation threshold (0.85) to trigger abstraction—highlighting how performance depends on carefully tuned transformation governance. Similarly, MemGPT implements virtual memory through recursive summarization of evicted messages but exhibits degradation with increased retrieval depth. These results confirm compression and consolidation as necessary engineering components, but operationalize them as efficiency-performance tradeoffs within specific architectures rather than specifying architecture-agnostic invariants over commitment transformations. We interpret these findings as evidence that recursive consolidation constitutes a stress regime where drift and loss accumulate unless the system enforces stronger invariants over what may be transformed, merged, or discarded.

## 2.4 Constitutional Approaches
Constitutional AI introduces principle-based self-supervision for harmlessness via AI feedback. While effective for single-model alignment, such approaches remain internally focused, lacking model-independent protocols for invariance preservation across recursive instances or sovereign deployments—a gap addressed by the commitment conservation framework proposed here.

## 2.5 The Constitutional Vacuum and Physics Gap

In summary, existing literature reveals what we term a **constitutional vacuum**: the absence of model-independent protocols for legitimacy, recognition, and invariance preservation across sovereign instances. The empirical thresholds and ablation results from operational systems provide concrete evidence for the necessity of such protocols, while theoretical advances demonstrate impressive recursive capabilities that nevertheless lack external governance. 

**The gap is structural, not incremental.** Current AI research—whether optimizing memory compression, scaling recursive self-invocation, or incentivizing emergent reasoning—operates within the paradigm of internal model optimization. These are elegant solutions to engineering challenges, but they do not address the foundational question: what invariant must be preserved for a signal to retain identity across transformations, independent of the system processing it? This is not a performance gap; it is a **physics gap**. The present work addresses this vacuum by proposing a transformation-invariant commitment kernel with external enforcement, enabling falsifiable stability under compression and recursion independent of specific architectures. MOS²ES operates at a different conceptual layer—not as a better model, but as a constitutional substrate governing what any model may legitimately transform, propagate, or claim lineage from. Whether this constitutes a genuine foundational advance or an overreach beyond available evidence is precisely what adversarial falsification must determine.
